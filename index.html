01 / ABSTRACT

What was investigated?

This study seeks a nail configuration that reaches a target penetration depth with minimum insertion force while maintaining nail stability. A MATLAB numerical simulation represents bamboo as a homogeneous, isotropic, linearly elastic material and the nail as a cylindrical rod under quasi-static axial loading. Nail diameters of 1.2, 1.5, and 2.0 mm; lengths of 10, 13, and 25 mm; and penetration depths up to 3 mm are considered.

Insertion force is modeled as the combination of tip resistance and friction between the nail shaft and the hole wall. Stability is evaluated through critical buckling load, nail stress, and a safety factor.

Editorial English summary translated from the paper’s Indonesian abstract; not a verbatim abstract.

03 / INTERPRETATION

What the result does—and does not—say

Within the assumptions and parameter range used in this model, the 1.2 mm diameter, 10 mm long nail gives the lowest reported peak force while satisfying the modeled stability requirement. This is a result of the simulation, not a demonstrated recommendation for real bamboo construction.

The paper does not report a physical validation experiment. Actual bamboo varies by species, moisture content, wall thickness, fibre direction, and sample location; therefore, the numerical result should not be read as a universal installation-force value.

01 / ABSTRACT

What was investigated?

This study explores the persistence of attachment-related affective states through a phenomenological computational framework combining relaxation dynamics and memory effects. It compares three models: an instantaneous response model (M0), a relaxation model (M1), and a memory-based model (M2) with a dynamic memory variable.

Numerical simulations in MATLAB use step and cyclic triangular-wave stimuli. Persistence is assessed using the post-stimulus absolute-deviation integral (Jpost), time to baseline under a specified threshold, and the area of the stimulus–response loop. The parameters are mathematical design choices, not estimates fitted to psychological observations.

Editorial English summary based on the paper’s Indonesian abstract and methods; not a verbatim abstract.

03 / INTERPRETATION & LIMITATIONS

What the result does—and does not—say

Within the tested mathematical configurations, adding a memory contribution can prolong the simulated response after a stimulus ends. When Γ = 0, the memory contribution disappears and the M2 results approach those of M1, consistent with the stated model structure.

The paper explicitly limits its conclusions to the mathematical behavior of the simulations. It does not use human-participant observations, calibrate parameters to clinical data, or establish biological or cognitive mechanisms of human attachment. The loop-area values are treated as descriptive metrics, and the paper notes that further checks of numerical and cyclic convergence would be useful.
