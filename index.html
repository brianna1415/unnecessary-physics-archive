01 / ABSTRACT

What was investigated?

This study seeks a nail configuration that reaches a target penetration depth with minimum insertion force while maintaining nail stability. A MATLAB numerical simulation represents bamboo as a homogeneous, isotropic, linearly elastic material and the nail as a cylindrical rod under quasi-static axial loading. Nail diameters of 1.2, 1.5, and 2.0 mm; lengths of 10, 13, and 25 mm; and penetration depths up to 3 mm are considered.

Insertion force is modeled as the combination of tip resistance and friction between the nail shaft and the hole wall. Stability is evaluated through critical buckling load, nail stress, and a safety factor.

Editorial English summary translated from the paper’s Indonesian abstract; not a verbatim abstract.

The paper reports that the modeled peak force increases with nail diameter and linearly with penetration depth. Nail length does not change the modeled insertion force, but longer nails have lower critical buckling loads. All nine diameter–length combinations meet the paper’s stated stability criterion under the simulation assumptions.

03 / INTERPRETATION

What the result does—and does not—say

Within the assumptions and parameter range used in this model, the 1.2 mm diameter, 10 mm long nail gives the lowest reported peak force while satisfying the modeled stability requirement. This is a result of the simulation, not a demonstrated recommendation for real bamboo construction.

The paper does not report a physical validation experiment. Actual bamboo varies by species, moisture content, wall thickness, fibre direction, and sample location; therefore, the numerical result should not be read as a universal installation-force value.
